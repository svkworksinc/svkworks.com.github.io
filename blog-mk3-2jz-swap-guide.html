MK3 Supra 2JZ Swap: Wiring Considerations

The 2JZ swap into the MK3 Supra is one of the most popular JDM builds. Here's what you need to know about the wiring side of this swap.

← Back to Blog

The MK3 Supra with a 2JZ swap is a legendary combination. But the wiring can be the most challenging part of the build if you don't plan ahead. The A70 chassis predates the 2JZ by nearly a decade, meaning nothing plugs in directly.

What You Need to Consider

  • ECU choice and connector pinout — Your ECU dictates the entire harness architecture. A standalone ECU is strongly recommended over converting a factory 2JZ harness.
  • Gauge cluster integration — The MK3 cluster uses different speedo, tach, temp, and fuel sender signals than the 2JZ. These need to be translated or replaced.
  • AC and power steering retention — If you want to keep these creature comforts, they require separate integration work on the engine harness.
  • Starter and charging system differences — The 2JZ starter and alternator wiring differs from the 7M-GE it replaced. A fresh harness handles these differences cleanly.

The OEM Conversion Trap

Many builders try to splice a 2JZ-GTE or 2JZ-GE factory harness into the MK3's existing chassis wiring. This usually works — until it doesn't. You end up with a harness that's half A70 era, half JZA80 era, full of splices and adaptors. When something fails, tracing the fault becomes a nightmare.
